Nguyen follows in footsteps of Tustin's Lumbard, attempts "power grab"

Last Wednesday, community members in Westminster expressed outrage at the mayor Chi Charlie M. Nguyen’s attempt to rush committee and board appointments before stepping down in December.

Staff report: "Appointment of Council Member to Represent the City on the Orange County Fire Authority for 2027-2029"
Staff report: "Consideration of Appointments to Council Ad Hoc Committee and Liaison Roles"

Just one day prior, residents of Tustin turned out to voice their opposition against their mayor’s attempts to rush such appointments. Tustin mayor Austin Lumbard—who sort of has a resemblance to Neal McDonough—was endorsed by the Republican Party and had just lost his re-election bid to Dr. Letitia Clark, who was endorsed by the Democratic Party. He reversed the appointments during closed session and was absent from the open session, citing death threats to him and his family.

Likewise, mayor Nguyen lost his re-election bid to 2nd council district member Carlos Manzo, who was endorsed by the Democratic Party. The Republican Party declined to re-endorse mayor Nguyen. Mayor-elect Manzo will be sworn in to the city council on Wednesday, 2nd December 2026.

But before Manzo can take office, mayor Nguyen has been accused of clinging onto power by residents and activists. A power grab, essentially. Legislative committee and board appointments are normally reserved for the incoming legislative body, i.e. city council.

A seat on the Orange County Fire Authority (O.C.F.A.) Board of Directors, the city’s Blakey Park ad hoc committee (a.h.c.), a liaison role for the Little Saigon Archway project, and two liaison roles for the Westminster 2027 Tet Parade were the appointments up for discussion last week.

Mayor Nguyen claimed the appointment to the O.C.F.A. Board was urgent because he had submitted his resignation from the board less than 24 hours before the council meeting. A new chief was already selected, and the Board was to confirm him the day after the council meeting. Mayor Nguyen reasoned that the city could not miss the confirmation of the chief.

Staff report: "Resignation from OCFA Board of Directors"

With his exit from the city council, he will no longer have a role on the city’s Blakey Park a.h.c. and as a liaison for the parade. He was therefore compelled to have a councilmember replace him. He did not explain why the archway project needed new liaisons before the swearing-in.

Manzo and outgoing councilmember Amy Phan West of the 1st council district were very vocal about waiting until Manzo and member-elect David Johnson were sworn in. Phan West consistently repeated that “it was the right thing to do”. But mayor Nguyen insisted that his job was not done until his last day as mayor.

Johnson, who was endorsed by the Democratic Party, will be replacing Phan West and sworn in with Manzo in December.


Substantial community opposition to the appointments

The council chamber was over-capacity, and unseated attendees were told to stand in the vestibule. Even then, some attendees spilled outside of building and watched a live broadcast of the meeting.

Just before moving onto oral communications, mayor Nguyen moved to reduce the speaking time from three to two minutes. The attendees expressed their disapproval. Manzo was the only dissenting vote.

The oral communications spanned more than four hours. Member-elect for the 70th Paula Swift and member-elect for Lake Forest’s 4th council district Amy Stevens voiced their opposition to the appointments in person. Clark and Aliso Viejo councilmember Tiffany Ackley voiced their opposition via teleconference.

Democratic Party of Orange County chair Florice O. Hoffman was interrupted by former candidate for California’s 41st congressional district Shonique Williams while speaking in opposition. Williams accused Hoffman of denying the existence of the Palestinian genocide.

When it was William’s turn to speak at the podium, she started by labelling Phan West as a pedophile-supporter, referring to PotUS Donald J. Trump. She continued her shouting towards Hoffman regarding the Palestinian genocide. Mayor Nguyen attempts at reminding Williams to direct her comments to the city council were met with profane language and an accusation of violating the Brown Act.

California Republican Assembly vice president George Grachen reminded mayor Nguyen why he did not receive an endorsement from the party. Cultural arts commissioner Alex Vaz, who was appointed by Phan West, and Jeff Pearlman of TheTruthOC.com opposed the appointments.

One teleconference speaker played an AI-rendition of Donald Trump, concisely letting mayor Nguyen know that he sucked. One speaker in Oregon waited hours to be able to let the city council know their opposition to the appointments. Many speakers talked about how it was embarrassing to live in a city that named a street after Charlie Kirk.

Williams was not the only speaker to use profane language in their comments. A majority of the speakers used a plethora of obscenities, which compelled Westminster resident Tom Huston to drive to the council meeting and engaged in verbal arguments with attendees about decorum.

There is a rule against the use of profane language in the council chamber, though mayor Nguyen appeared too exhausted and defeated to enforce the rule.

Outside the council chamber, Vietnamese media who supported Manzo and two Vietnam war activists who supported mayor Nguyen were engaged in verbal altercations. The activists accused the media of being Communist sympathizers and bootlickers, calling them bastards and dogs, and cursing their mothers.

One of the activists, Kim Van, screamed in Vietnamese that the media were “letting the Mexicans take advantage of the Vietnamese community”. She denied accusations of racism from one member of the media.


Appointment to the O.C.F.A. Board of Directors

Manzo asked O.C.F.A. division chief Craig Covey whether Westminster’s absence from the board meeting would hinder the confirmation of the newly-selected chief. Covey stated that 13 members needed to attend and there were 14 members were planning to attend. Manzo concluded that there was no need for an appointment to the board and seconded Phan West’s motion to take no action.

Mayor Nguyen made a substitute motion for vice mayor Mark Nguyen (no relation to mayor Nguyen) to sit in for the time being, to which he declined. Councilmember NamQuan Nguyen (no relation to mayor Nguyen and vice mayor Nguyen) also declined an offer to sit-in and directed it to Manzo. Manzo declined, reasoning that member-elect Johnson deserved to have a say in the appointments.

Phan West’s motion passed with no dissenting votes.


Tet Parade liaison replacement

Since the Tet Parade was lifted off the shoulder of private entities and onto city staff, it has become one of the city’s most arduous projects, yearly. This why mayor Nguyen felt compelled to have a councilmember replace him as a liaison. He reasoned that the liaisons reduce the staff workload, though this has been highly debated.

In response to Phan West, city manager Christine Cordon said that city staff would be able to pull off a parade with or without liaisons.

Councilmember Nguyen motioned to have vice mayor Nguyen join him as a liaison for the parade. Vice mayor Nguyen did not understand why it was so controversial to have liaisons for the parade appointed, and then accused Manzo of not financially supporting the parade.

Nguyen, Nguyen, and Nguyen—referred to as the Nguyen dynasty by some—voted affirmatively, while Phan West and Manzo dissented.


Blakey Park ad hoc committee member replacement

Manzo questioned the viability of the Blakey Park a.h.c. Assistant city manager Adolfo Ozaeta clarified that the committee reports back to the city council. Manzo noted it hasn’t reported anything, and that staff should take over the role of the committee. Councilmember Nguyen countered that “not yet doesn’t mean never”.

Phan West urged the councilmembers to take no action, once again. She explained that she and mayor Nguyen were exiting the council, and thus these appointments should be made when Manzo and Johnson are sworn-in. Manzo seconded Phan West’s motion.

Councilmember Nguyen made a substitute motion to have vice mayor Nguyen join him on the committee, which didn’t help with the Nguyen dynasty rumor. The meeting attendees became disruptive and responded with unsurprised shock and laughter.

As the attendees became increasingly rowdy, councilmember Nguyen pointed at one attendee and ordered them to “shut up”. He had actually done it once earlier, though it went unnoticed. The attendees begain shouting and pointing at him, and mayor Nguyen recessed the meeting. Throughout the shouting and pointing, councilmember Nguyen smiled and laughed at the attendees.

After reconvening, the councilmembers voted 4 to 1 on the substitute motion. Manzo was the lone dissenting vote “just for the sake of principle”. Phan West had actually voted yes despite motioning to take no action. She reasoned that “this was not a hill we needed to die over” and that the community “got a win already”.


Little Saigon Archway project liaison appointments

Manzo stressed that the archway was supposed to be a community project. He felt liaisons shouldn’t be solely in charge of the archway because it would hinder transparency and motioned to take no action. The motion died on the floor.

Phan West refuted the claim that a liaison would hinder transparency, though she agreed that the appointment should wait until the swearing-in. She declared that she was therefore neutral on the subject.

Mayor Nguyen motioned to appoint Nguyen and Nguyen as liaisons, which was responded with uproar. This led him to ultimately kick out two attendees, who happened to be Manzo’s wife and sister-in-law.

The motion died on the floor. Manzo and Phan West simultaneously moved to take no action. Manzo dropped his motion and seconded Phan West’s. Just when things were already pretty confusing, mayor Nguyen then made a substitute motion to also take no action. Phan West comedically threw her hands up in the air and exclaimed, “okay, I second you, Mr. Mayor! Woo!” The vote was unanimous and the motion passed.


Battle over mayoral powers continues

The matter was settled after more than seven gruelling meeting hours at half past 1 in the morning, until agenda item N°. 10 (requests for agenda items from city council members).

Vice mayor Nguyen moved to agendize a resolution by which a certificate of recognition would not need mayoral approval. Phan West seconded the motion, and Manzo was the lone dissenter. Councilmember Nguyen was absent.

Then Phan West moved to place a discussion regarding the mayor’s ability to agendize an item without a vote. She explained that the current arrangement “gives certain positions more authority than others”.

Pearlman stated on his website that “the Republicans know precisely what they’re doing”. He theorised that the purpose of the change to the arrangement was to Manzo’s ability to agendize an item “handcuffed and limited” because only two Democrats would be on the city council.

The motion passed with councilmember Nguyen absent and Manzo being the lone dissenter.

The city council will discuss and vote on these items at the next meeting on Monday, 30th November.
