Municipal midterm races two weeks after Election Day

City of Westminster

As previously covered, outgoing councilmember Amy Phan West conceded the race for Westminster's 1st council district a week after election day. On 10th November, she came 2nd place with 41.14% of the votes, while her opponent David Johnson defeated her with an absolute majority. Johnson indicated hope with the results just two days after election day, and declared victory five days after.

Westminster's mayoral race has also been decided. With 60.8% voters turning out and still counting, Carlos Manzo declared himself "mayor-elect" of Westminster. He received a plurality of the votes, 41.13%. His opponent Chi Charlie M. Nguyen has not yet conceded the race. Initially in 1st place, Nguyen's lead vanished a couple of days after Election Day; he has 37.19% of the votes. In 3rd place with 17.17% of the votes, former councilmember Kimberly Ho—who ran for mayor in 2024 as well—has already conceded the race. She indicated that she would be available to represent the 2nd council district if it opens.

The race for Westminster's 4th council district has not yet completed. NamQuan Nguyen and Loan Nguyen are the top contenders for the seat. As of 10th November, N. Nguyen has a plurality with 38.52% of the votes and L. Nguyen is in 2nd place with 33.35% of the votes. Neither have declared a loss or win thus far, although L. Nguyen has expressed hope after the gap slowly began closing between her and N. Nguyen. Kelly Daniels (Kieu Quan), trailing far behind L. Nguyen, has not commented on the results.

City of Huntington Beach

The City of Westminster adopted a district-based council ordinance in October 2019 after it was accused of violating the California Voting Rights Act (C.V.R.A.). The city was initially opposed, but elected not to fight the accusations, citing legal fees. In November 2020, the city was districted.

Staff report: "California Voting Rights Act — Demand for District Elections; Consideration of Whether to Transition from an At-Large Electoral System to a District-Based Electoral System for Members of the City Council"

Meanwhile, the City of Huntington Beach chose to fight the State to continue at-large voting. As a result, an Orange County judge ordered—in June 2026—the city to implement ranked-choice voting (R-C.V.) by this year's midterm general elections. Still, at-large voting remained because the Orange County Registrar of Voters did not yet have a way to implement R-C.V.

Nine relevant candidates are fighting for a seat on the H.B. City Council. Asterisks indicate incumbents.

  • *Pat Burns, elected 2022, endorsed by Republican Party
  • Ben Davis, endorsed by Democratic Party
  • Brenda Glim, endorsed by Democratic Party
  • *Andrew Gruel, appointed 2025, endorsed by Republican Party
  • *Casey McKeon, elected 2022, endorsed. by Republican Party
  • Erin Spivey, endorsed by Democratic Party
  • Taryn Taddeo Palumbo, endorsed by Democratic Party
  • Brian Thienes, endorsed by Republican Party

In 2022, H.B. lost its 5-2 Democratic majority to Burns, McKeon, Tony Strickland (replaced by Gruel), and Gracey van der Mark. The final three H.B. Democrats were ousted in 2023 by Don Kennedy, Butch Twining, and Chad Williams. The supermajority is colloquially labelled "MAGA-nificient 7".

As of 13th November, McKeon, Burns, Gruel, and Davis—in decreasing order of the number of votes—are frontrunners for city council, with 2,576 votes between McKeon and Davis. McKeon received 37,932 votes; Davis is trailing Gruel by just 43 votes. Spivey is trailing Davis by 63 votes. Glim is in last place of these candidates, with 3,551 votes between her and Davis.

Throughout the ballot counting, Davis has remained in 4th place. He hopes that he will be able to close the gap between him and Gruel, and that Spivey will then overtake Gruel. Likewise, McKeon, Burns, and Gruel have held on to their respective places. The three have not commented on the current results.

With an estimated 15,680 ballots left to process, the turnout for this year's midterms so far is 60.8%. It is lower than the Presidential General Election in 2024 at 76.1%, which is to be expected. Compared to the Midterm General Election in 2022 at 54,7%, this year's turnout was marginally higher. The 2018 Midterm General Election saw a turnout of 71.0%.

The 2020 Presidential General Election saw the highest voter turnout of 87.3% in Orange County history and in California. The 2014 Midterm General Election had the lowest voter turnout, 45.0%.
